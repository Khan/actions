Medium-importance findings no longer demote an approval. A run where nothing blocks now submits APPROVE; medium findings still post inline ahead of minor ones, and the approval head counts them: "**✅ Approved** — nothing blocks; 1 finding worth fixing before merge (see inline comments)." (or "N findings", pointing at the observations fold when the mediums collapsed there, or at both surfaces when they split). A conditional approval appends the same clause after its pre-merge-obligations line. The bot's approval now means "no blocking findings", matching its own labels: a finding that should stop an approval is a blocking finding, and that bar is where an under-graded finding gets fixed. The precedence is REQUEST_CHANGES > HOLD_FOR_HUMAN > APPROVE; the hold is unchanged and still folds medium findings into the hold comment.

COMMENT remains for two cases, neither of them a verdict: a reduced-depth round whose would-be approval needs a full roster (unchanged, with its existing heads), and a canary run, which now always carries the would-be verdict note and a "canary run" head. The COMMENT-to-APPROVE upgrade over the bot's own prior request-changes review is removed: a full-roster verdict can no longer be COMMENT, so a full or scoped approval clears the block directly. The eval runner's replica feeds the medium count to its rendered head instead of its verdict, so its body matches the shipped path.

Expected output-shape effect: no change to inline comment count or comment bodies. The review event changes from COMMENT to APPROVE exactly when a full- or scoped-depth run found medium findings and nothing blocking (on one consumer, about 45% of such runs), and those reviews gain a one-line head where an approval with inline comments used to have an empty body. Step 7's risk/patterns comment, which runs only on a full-depth APPROVE, now also posts on the full-depth subset of those runs when there are risk files, patterns, or notified people to report. Step 8's reviewer requests now run on APPROVE only: a COMMENT is a demoted reduced-depth round (which Step 8 already skipped) or a canary, and neither should route the owning teams.
